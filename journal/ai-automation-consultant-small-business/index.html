How to hire an AI automation consultant without wasting money.

The best first AI project is usually not the flashiest. It is the annoying, repeatable job your team has to do again tomorrow.

If you're looking for an AI automation consultant, start with the work you want gone. Not the model, not a demo, and definitely not a promise that an agent will run your entire business.

SOVTIVA works with business owners and decision-makers to find costly manual work, design a practical fix, and, when appropriate, build, connect or maintain the system. We are tool-agnostic. Sometimes the right answer is automation; sometimes it is a simpler process. For larger custom software projects, we can help evaluate independent specialists through our partner network.

What should an AI automation consultant actually do?

A useful consultant should understand a workflow before proposing software. Ask them to show the trigger, inputs, owners, exceptions and definition of a correct result. Then they should tell you which parts need rules, which need an AI model, and which still need a person.

Three projects worth examining first

  • Inbox to action: classify incoming requests, collect missing information, assign an owner and create a traceable follow-up.
  • Document-heavy work: extract fields, compare records, prepare a draft and route uncertain cases for human review.
  • Reporting and handoffs: reconcile information across approved tools, flag exceptions and send the next person a clean summary.

These are examples of possible projects, not claims that SOVTIVA has delivered them for specific clients. Feasibility depends on the data, tools, permissions and review requirements.

Decide whether you need rules, an integration, an agent or a custom app.

  1. Fix the process first if people disagree about what should happen next. Software will only make the confusion faster.
  2. Use existing automation when the steps are predictable and your current tools already support them.
  3. Add AI carefully when the work requires interpreting language, documents or messy inputs. Keep checks around important decisions.
  4. Consider custom software when a system must serve several users, connect unusual tools, handle complex permissions or become a core product.

The point is not to buy the most advanced option. It is to remove enough work to justify the cost of building and maintaining it.

Seven questions to ask before you hire anyone.

  1. Can you show me a process map before you quote a build?
  2. Which systems will this touch, and what access does it need?
  3. What happens when the AI is unsure, wrong or offline?
  4. Who reviews sensitive actions and changes?
  5. Will we own our data, documentation and the relevant deliverables?
  6. How will we measure the baseline against the result?
  7. Who fixes it after launch, and what does that support cost?

If a provider cannot answer these questions at the appropriate level for your project, slow down before signing.

When you should not hire an AI consultant yet.

If nobody owns the process, nobody can provide sample cases, or nobody can review mistakes, prepare those basics first. You may also be better off with a built-in feature of software you already pay for. A responsible recommendation can be: don't build this yet.

Common questions

How much does business AI automation cost?

It depends on the number of systems, data quality, exception handling, security requirements, implementation work and support. We scope the job before proposing a price. A price without a process is a guess.

Do I need a custom AI agent?

Not necessarily. A rules-based integration may be cheaper and more reliable. Use an agent only where interpretation or adaptive steps create enough value to justify extra testing and oversight.

Can SOVTIVA handle the work directly?

For suitable workflow, integration, automation and lightweight-tool projects, yes, subject to scoping and capacity. For specialist development, we may recommend an independent provider and request your permission before any introduction.

Bring us the most annoying part of your week.

Two details are enough to start: what your team repeats, and what happens when it is late or wrong. We'll tell you whether it looks like a process fix, an automation or a larger build.
