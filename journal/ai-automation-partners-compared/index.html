How to choose an AI automation partner. Start with the job, not the logo.

A custom software studio, an industry-specific AI platform and a hands-on automation operator are not interchangeable. The right choice depends on what needs to work on Monday morning.

First, which problem are you buying a solution for?

  • Your team repeats work across existing tools: you may need process cleanup, an integration or a small automation before you need a new application.
  • You need a new product or complex internal system: a custom software team may be appropriate.
  • You need a role-specific operational teammate: evaluate vendors that understand the actual industry workflow, not just generic chat interfaces.
  • You aren't sure yet: pay attention to who can define a useful first project before asking you to buy technology.

sovtiva: a first stop for scoping and practical implementation.

Possible fit: a founder, executive or team lead has manual work across spreadsheets, email, documents or business systems and wants someone to turn that mess into a useful workflow. sovtiva helps identify the opportunity, plan the change and, where suitable, configure, integrate, automate or build a lightweight tool. Work can be structured as a one-off project, a sprint or continued support.

A quick way to choose who to speak with.

  • "Our approvals and reports live in five tools." Begin with a workflow review; sovtiva may help directly.
  • "We need a custom platform or serious AI integration." Evaluate a dedicated software provider such as Forcoda.
  • "Our HVAC dispatchers are buried in intake and scheduling." Evaluate Pureblue's actual supported HVAC workflows.
  • "Our growing business needs its own internal app." Evaluate custom engineering, including Larsen Code Labs.

These are starting points, not exclusive recommendations. A discovery conversation may show that none of these is the right fit.

Ask every provider these six questions.

  1. What is included in discovery, and what will we receive before development?
  2. What real systems, users and edge cases does the solution support?
  3. What are the human review, security and failure-handling controls?
  4. Who owns the data, accounts, source code and documentation?
  5. What is the complete implementation and support model?
  6. What simple measurable result would prove the pilot worked?

There is no honest universal answer. Ask who can demonstrate fit for your workflow, evidence for their capabilities, appropriate controls and clear support terms. The right provider for a complex AI product may be the wrong one for a simple back-office integration.
