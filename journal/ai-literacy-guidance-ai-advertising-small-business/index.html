Knowing how to use AI is becoming the new knowing Excel.

Everyone has access to increasingly capable AI. Not everyone knows what to ask it to do, how to connect it to real work, or when to stop it. That gap is about to matter a lot.

There was a time when knowing how to use a computer was a job skill. Then came Excel. A person who understood formulas, filters and a decent spreadsheet could do work that took everyone else half a day.

Most people did not need to become programmers. They needed to learn how to use the machine to solve the problems already sitting on their desks.

AI is entering that stage now. Learning how to work with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and AI agents may become as ordinary a business skill as knowing how to use email or Excel. The bigger opportunity is not memorizing prompt tricks. It is learning to direct the tools toward a useful result.

“AI is only as smart as the person using it.” True? Almost.

It is a great line, with one important correction. A model can know more about accounting rules, programming or ad platforms than the person asking the question. Its underlying capabilities do not change because the user is inexperienced.

What changes is the value you can get out of it. Someone who understands a business, describes the right problem, gives the model useful context and checks the result is much more likely to turn AI capability into something worth using.

Ask an AI to “make us more sales” and it can produce pages of impressive-sounding nothing. Tell it which customers you serve, what a qualified lead looks like, what your margins allow, what you have tried, and which decisions must stay with you, and the conversation becomes a working brief.

One person gets a generic answer. Another gets a draft process, a testable plan and a list of assumptions to verify. Same category of software. Different level of direction.

The research points beyond better prompting.

A field study of 5,179 customer support workers found that access to a generative AI assistant increased issues resolved per hour by 14% on average, with bigger gains among less experienced workers. That is evidence from one workplace setting, not a promise for every business.

Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index adds a useful twist. Its analysis of surveyed AI users found organizational conditions such as management support, culture and AI practices were more strongly associated with reported AI outcomes than individual mindset and behavior. Microsoft describes associations, not proof of cause and effect.

Put the two together and the lesson is practical: teaching one employee to write nicer prompts is a start. Giving a team a safe, repeatable way to use AI inside its actual work is where the idea becomes a business system.

Look at AI advertising. The tools are changing right now.

Google Ads and Meta already provide a preview of what this shift means. Google's AI Max for Search can expand matching beyond a manually chosen keyword list, adapt ad text using account and landing-page material, and select relevant landing pages under advertiser controls.

On October 6, 2026, Meta described new AI advertising features that help marketers inspect creative performance and, in limited testing, prepare campaigns conversationally. Draft-to-publish workflows remain subject to permissions and explicit approval. Not every announced function is available to every advertiser.

That sounds like the ads should run themselves. They shouldn't. The platforms can generate variations and optimize delivery. They still need a business to tell them what a good customer is, which claims are accurate, which audiences are appropriate, what it can spend, and what outcome matters.

A small example: a local cleaning business

Say the owner wants more recurring office-cleaning contracts, not one-time apartment jobs.

  • Without direction: “Make five Facebook ads for my cleaning company.” The AI writes five polished but interchangeable ads.
  • With direction: “We sell weekly office cleaning to businesses within our service area. No residential jobs. Our advantage is reliable scheduling. Draft three distinct angles, note any claims that need proof, and suggest a simple landing page. Do not publish or change ad budgets.”
  • With a system: The business reviews the drafts, approves the creative and budget, runs a controlled test in its ad account and compares qualified inquiries and booked calls, not just clicks.

AI can make more options cheaper to produce. It cannot decide whether those options reflect your real offer, or whether the people clicking them are worth serving.

Five things AI guidance can unlock beyond ads.

  • Sales and lead follow-up: organize approved prospect data, prepare relevant outreach drafts, flag missing CRM information and help a person decide who needs a reply.
  • Social media: turn a founder's ideas into a content calendar, draft posts in a consistent voice, prepare visual briefs and route everything through review before scheduling.
  • Customer inquiries: structure intake forms, appointment requests, WhatsApp handoffs and reminders so fewer requests disappear in a crowded inbox.
  • Websites and digital tools: build or update a website with AI-assisted workflows, connect forms to a spreadsheet or CRM, and let owners review changes before they go live.
  • Everyday operations: transform messy spreadsheets into checklists, spot repeatable tasks and document how to handle exceptions instead of relying on the one employee who remembers everything.

Each job requires different permissions, data controls and review steps. Sensitive customer information should not be pasted into an unapproved AI service. Ads, messages, financial decisions and website changes need human accountability.

What does being good at AI actually mean?

Forget the 200-line “ultimate prompt.” Start with five ordinary habits:

The AI working brief

  1. Outcome: What should be different when the task is done?
  2. Context: Who is this for? What data, tools and prior work matter?
  3. Boundaries: What may the AI read, draft or change? What needs approval?
  4. Proof: How will you check facts, quality, privacy and business fit?
  5. Next step: What happens after the first draft, and who owns it?

OpenAI's own prompting guidance recommends clear tasks, useful context and iteration. Good judgment also means recognizing when a confident answer is wrong.

What used to be “Can you make a spreadsheet?” may soon sound like “Can you use an AI agent to finish this task, show me what it changed and prove it worked?”

Do you need an AI consultant, or can you learn this yourself?

Both are valid. If the job is a one-off draft, start learning with the tools you already have. If several people repeat the same task, data moves between apps, or changes could affect customers or spend, a structured setup is usually worth considering.

Quick questions people ask.

Is AI literacy the next basic office skill?

That is our prediction, not a universal hiring requirement today. As AI becomes part of common business software, knowing how to brief, check and supervise it should become increasingly useful.

Can ChatGPT or an AI agent manage Google Ads and Meta Ads?

AI can assist with research, creative drafts, campaign setup and analysis. Specific platform features, integration permissions and account availability vary. Keep campaign spending, publishing and policy compliance under authorized human control.

What is the difference between AI training and AI advisory?

Training helps people learn the tools. Advisory also helps choose a business use case, design the workflow, set boundaries and decide whether automation or a technical build is appropriate.

How can a small business start using AI at work?

Pick one repeated task, write down the current steps, decide what the AI may do, review its output and measure whether the change genuinely saves effort or improves quality before expanding.

Our take.

The last generation learned to use computers. Then spreadsheets. Then search. This generation will learn to direct software that can take action.

AI does not become smarter because you ask better questions. Your work can become smarter because you know what to ask, what to trust and what to do next. That is a skill worth learning before everyone starts putting it on their résumé.
