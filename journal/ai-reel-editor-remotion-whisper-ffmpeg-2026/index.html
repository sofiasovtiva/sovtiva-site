Sovtiva / Code-driven production and business economics / November 11, 2026

A prompt isn't
an editing studio.

Remotion, FFmpeg and Whisper can remove repetitive editing work. We examine the real stack, the failure points and the business case without pretending one prompt can do it all.

Sovtiva ResearchNovember 11, 2026Documented tools; implementation untested

A polished reel contains dozens of small decisions: where a breath becomes a cut, which word deserves emphasis, whether a moving crop feels natural, and whether Instagram's interface covers the caption. Esteban Pérez (@estebanperez.ia) published a code-first recipe for automating much of this. The underlying tools are real. The commercial question is how much editorial work remains afterward.

The original Spanish guide describes a talking-head reel edited with Claude Code, Remotion, FFmpeg and whisper.cpp: transcription, silence cuts, speaker reframing, word-synced captions, scripted animations and final quality control. That is a useful published workflow, not independent evidence that every source recording becomes a client-ready reel with one prompt.

The software behind the claim

01 / WHISPER.CPPTranscribe words
02 / FFMPEGTrim and mix
03 / REMOTIONAnimate and render
04 / EDITORCheck and approve

Whisper.cpp transcribes local audio and offers experimental word-level timing; names, trailing syllables and unusual phrases still need review. FFmpeg applies repeatable cuts, crops and audio processing. Remotion uses React code to position captions and motion elements at precise frames. Face tracking can be added with systems such as MediaPipe. These tools can help make a template reusable rather than rebuilding the same title animation for every client.

But an automated cut can remove a syllable. A face-tracking crop can overreact. A correctly timed caption can still sit behind a platform button. The source recipe proposes thresholds such as 0.3 seconds of silence, 1080 × 1920 resolution, 30 fps and −14 LUFS audio. Those are parameters to evaluate, not universal requirements independently verified for Instagram.

Does this require Claude?

No. The publicly shared recipe happens to use Claude Code as the programming assistant. The actual media pipeline is built from scripts and video tools. Sovtiva's implementation would use ChatGPT and original code, with versioned Remotion templates and explicit review stages. We would not republish the creator's prompt verbatim or imply that his workflow is our product.

Successful delivery still requires compatible hardware, working dependencies, source files, appropriate rights, and an actual render-and-review test. Sovtiva has researched the architecture but has not yet shipped or benchmarked its own editor.

Could an agency make money with it?

Potentially. A founder recording weekly thoughts, a podcaster with interviews, or a professional creating educational explainers may want consistent short-form distribution without managing editing software. The service is a finished asset with sound, captions, brand rules and timely review, not access to a prompt.

A deliberately hypothetical package

Take eight approved reels for $480 per month. At eight hours of work valued internally at $20 per hour, plus $20 in variable tooling costs, the project leaves $300 in contribution before sales work, extra revision cycles, taxes and overhead. That is a pricing scenario, not evidence of clients, actual editing speed or achieved margin. If revisions push the job to 24 hours, the economics look very different.

Automation is valuable only if it measurably reduces time per approved clip while maintaining useful quality. A one-off demo with authorized footage will tell us more about that than dozens of speculative video prompts.

Licenses and real costs

FFmpeg and whisper.cpp can be used without purchasing an all-in-one video editor subscription, subject to their software terms. Remotion currently permits free commercial use by individuals and businesses with up to three employees; bigger organizations may need a paid license. Remotion also warns of license changes with version 5.0. Hosting, compute, transcription models, music rights, revisions and client onboarding should be priced separately.

The original guide requires a paid Claude Code plan for its workflow. That subscription is not a fundamental requirement of FFmpeg, Remotion or Whisper. Sovtiva would not purchase any additional tools without owner approval.

Do not skip the final watch. Inspect captions in a small phone view, listen through cuts, check claims, compare source and edited speech, review safe areas, and document image, music and recording rights. A contact sheet is useful, but not a substitute for watching the full export.

A programmable editor becomes valuable when it removes repeat work, while a human keeps responsibility for meaning and quality.

What we would validate first

  1. Obtain one source video owned by Sovtiva or expressly licensed for editing.
  2. Build one original 25–50 second vertical reel, with word-timed captions and a restrained brand template.
  3. Measure generation, render, review and revision time instead of guessing.
  4. Compare against a manual edit of the same source footage, without paid tools.
  5. Package a bounded deliverable for buyers already recording long-form content. No promised virality.

This model can complement Ssemble-style clipping tools and brand-funded paid-view campaigns. They are different businesses: making clips efficiently does not mean a platform will pay for them.
