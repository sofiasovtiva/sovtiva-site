You were missing
from one answer.

A viral sales template opens with a competitor in a ChatGPT answer. The format works as a curiosity hook. Its credibility depends on the evidence behind that one answer, and on whether the sender can actually help.

Sovtiva ResearchNovember 12, 2026Source-backed editorial research

The sample email is elegantly small: ask an AI assistant for the best product in a category, show the prospect that competitors appeared and offer to share the sources. You can understand the appeal. It speaks about a buyer moment the company may be missing.

The problem is equally small. One generated answer is not a market-share report. The valuable service begins when you can explain what happened, reproduce the observation, and decide what a business can responsibly change.

What the screenshot proves, and what it doesn't

A timestamped screenshot can show that, for one phrasing of one question, a particular model or search experience included specific companies and links. It does not prove that everyone sees the same answer, that the prospect's competitors dominate the category, that the named brand never appears elsewhere, or that a search engine endorses the omitted business.

Even the post behind the screenshot makes a useful cautionary point: when the same cold email format spreads widely, it loses its distinctiveness. A sender who simply replaces two competitor names in a viral script is no longer demonstrating special research.

The audit worth sending

Start with buyer questions, not brand vanity searches. For a small-business automation provider these might include “best agency to connect WhatsApp and CRM for a clinic in Mexico” or “who can build a lightweight lead intake workflow.” The phrasing should reflect a real purchasable service and a credible geography.

01 / OBSERVE

Record the run

Save prompt, engine, date, location or language context, answer, linked sources and caveats.

02 / REPEAT

Check variability

Use several relevant questions and fresh runs; preserve missing and conflicting results.

03 / TRACE

Read the sources

Distinguish a cited article that actually names a company from a source merely linked nearby.

04 / RECOMMEND

Find a fixable gap

Check crawlability, missing buyer pages, poor service details, original evidence and legitimate third-party coverage.

Track brand mentioned, site cited, competitor named, and qualified referral separately. A citation is not the same as a recommendation, and a recommendation is not the same as a paying customer.

What the platforms actually say

Google's guidance says traditional search foundations still apply to AI features: useful, original content; pages that can be crawled and indexed; and honest representation of a business. Google expressly warns against “GEO hacks” such as artificial mentions or unnecessary AI-targeted files.

For ChatGPT Search, OpenAI documents the OAI-SearchBot crawler. Search eligibility can be affected by crawl settings. Neither bot access nor adding structured data guarantees a mention or citation. The strongest defensible recommendation is to improve the precise information a real buyer needs to compare providers.

A responsible first email has to stand on its own

“For this specific question, two companies appeared and yours did not. I checked the pages that were cited. One answers the buyer's pricing question clearly; your current page doesn't. I can share the comparison.”

That is an illustrative structure, not a claim about any real prospect. It is usable only when the sender actually checked the result, the sources and the recipient's page. It earns credibility by naming a concrete and fixable gap. A fabricated audit, fake client success story or invented source list destroys the offer before it starts.

Commercial email also has compliance requirements. For US recipients, the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide requires accurate sender and subject information, a valid postal address and a working opt-out path for applicable commercial messages. Local privacy and marketing rules can add obligations. An audit is not a license to scrape private contact data or ignore opt-outs.

What Sovtiva could sell here

A practical commercial offer is a small, evidence-led AI search visibility review for companies with clear service categories. Deliverable: a scoped prompt set, dated findings, source map, accessibility and content gaps, three prioritized improvements, and a follow-up measurement plan. The customer receives useful work before buying a content retainer.

There is a sensible second engagement: implement the improvements. That may mean clearer service pages, credible comparisons, useful original demonstrations, technical indexing checks or better lead capture. The provider must not guarantee placement. Some improvements are under the client's control; recommendations produced by AI systems are not.

How we would measure the result

Maintain a baseline by prompt and engine; inspect dated repeat runs; check Google Search Console, Bing webmaster data where available, referral traffic and qualified inquiries. A published page, a link in an answer and a new customer belong to different parts of the funnel. This research proposes an operating method; Sovtiva has not yet delivered a paid visibility audit or verified a lift attributable to it.
