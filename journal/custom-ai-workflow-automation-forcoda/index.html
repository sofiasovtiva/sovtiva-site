Custom AI automation or another SaaS tool? A practical way to decide.

An automation project can fail before anyone writes code. The first question is whether the work needs a new tool, a cleaner process, or a system built around how your business actually operates.

Start with the workflow, not the technology.

Write down what triggers the work, who touches it, what information they need, and what a correct result looks like. A process that changes every week usually needs clarification before automation. A stable process with repeatable exceptions may be ready for software.

Take a team processing customer requests from email, spreadsheets and an internal system. The problem may be missing ownership, not missing AI. A shared queue could solve it. But if every request also needs context from several systems, approval rules and an auditable handoff, a tailored workflow may deserve consideration.

Five questions that make the decision easier

  1. Does an existing tool cover most of the process? If yes, configure and test it before paying for custom development.
  2. Are the exceptions part of the business? Frequent, important exceptions can make generic automation brittle. Map them before estimating a build.
  3. Where does the data live? List the systems, permissions, records and owners. Integration constraints often decide feasibility.
  4. Who checks mistakes? AI output should have a review point wherever an incorrect decision would be expensive, sensitive or hard to reverse.
  5. What would count as a win? Pick one measurable outcome, such as turnaround time, rework or time spent on handoffs. Capture a baseline first.

What to bring to a discovery conversation

  • A rough process map and two or three real examples.
  • Systems that must exchange information.
  • Who owns the data and signs off on changes.
  • One outcome to measure in a small pilot.
  • Constraints on access, hosting and privacy.

When to wait.

If your process is undocumented, your data is unreliable or your team cannot identify an owner, fix those first. Automation can make a bad handoff happen faster. A good specialist should be willing to say when a custom build is premature.
