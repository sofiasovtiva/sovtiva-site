How do you track Etsy, Shopify and Gumroad profit in one spreadsheet?

Five dashboards can tell you how many sales you made. They are less helpful when you want to know what you actually kept.

Short answer: Use one sales log for every platform, then subtract actual refunds, payment and marketplace fees, product-specific costs and operating expenses. Keep the fee assumptions editable and check them against the real settlement reports. For a small digital shop, a disciplined spreadsheet may be enough; sovtiva also makes a dedicated Excel workbook for this job.

There is a reason sellers get this wrong: revenue arrives in different places, fees have different labels and the time you spent building a product rarely appears in the sales dashboard.

Start with five records, not a pretty chart.

  1. Products: product ID, title, price, launch date and where it is sold.
  2. Sales: order date, platform, currency, product, quantity, gross revenue, discounts and refunds.
  3. Fees: actual transaction, payment-processing, advertising and other platform charges, using the fee statement as evidence.
  4. Operating costs: software, contractors, assets and other business expenses with dates and notes.
  5. Review: a monthly check that ties the spreadsheet back to platform payouts and bank records.

Keep gross revenue, cash payouts and profit as separate numbers. They answer different questions. A payout is not automatically profit, and sales tax collected on behalf of an authority generally should not be mistaken for revenue you earned.

A simple profit calculation.

Net operating result = sales revenue − refunds − platform and payment fees − advertising − applicable product costs − operating expenses.

That is a management view, not a formal accounting statement. Treat VAT, sales tax, income tax and currency conversions according to your actual books and professional advice. Avoid mixing a one-time launch expense into every individual sale without a consistent allocation method.

If you sell in several currencies, record both the original amount and the conversion used for your reporting currency. Never silently add dollars, pesos and euros together.

What about fees? They change.

Do not copy a fee percentage from an old YouTube tutorial into a permanent formula. Etsy, Shopify and Gumroad have their own current pricing and fee rules, and actual charges can depend on region, payment method, plan and transaction details. Check their official pages and your account statements: Etsy fees, Shopify pricing and Gumroad pricing.

The durable design is a fee reference you can edit, with a place to enter actual charges when they differ from the estimate.

When a spreadsheet is enough, and when it isn't.

Stay with a workbook if your order volume is manageable, you can reconcile regularly and you mainly want product-level profitability and a monthly view. Consider accounting software or deeper integrations if you need inventory valuation, complex tax handling, high transaction volume, multiple entities or financial reporting with audit requirements.

Common questions

Can I track digital products across multiple stores in one workbook?

Yes. Use a consistent product ID and a platform column for each transaction, then group results by product, store and month.

Is an Excel tracker better than an accounting app?

It solves a different job. A tracker can make operating decisions easier; an accounting system may be required for formal records, reconciliation and tax responsibilities.
