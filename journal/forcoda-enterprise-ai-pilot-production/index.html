Why enterprise AI pilots stall at production, and what a serious delivery partner tests.

A prototype can perform beautifully on yesterday's data. Production is where records arrive late, permissions change, APIs fail and someone must know how to stop the system. Buyers should judge vendors by their answers to those failures.

The transition is an operating problem.

Forcoda's enterprise AI services explicitly target the gap between pilots and systems that work with real company data and software. Its public offering includes readiness assessment, opportunity discovery and implementation. That framing matters because most risks appear outside model accuracy: user rights, data quality, changed dependencies, human review and support after launch.

A project can “work” in a demonstration while silently using old documents, creating duplicate CRM entries or sending a confident answer to the wrong audience. A production gate has to test the workflow as a whole.

A six-part release gate.

  1. Scope: define one process and the specific action the model is or is not allowed to take.
  2. Evidence: assemble representative current, stale, contradictory and deliberately missing inputs.
  3. Identity: test least-privilege retrieval and action permissions for real roles.
  4. Evaluation: separate extraction errors, tool-use errors, unsupported claims and bad handoffs.
  5. Recovery: demonstrate logging, retry limits, deduplication, rollback and a manual route.
  6. Ownership: name the person who monitors costs, exceptions, quality and model changes.

These are Sovtiva's buyer checks, informed by the NIST AI RMF; they are not proprietary features proven to be present in every Forcoda engagement.

Ask for the embarrassing demo.

The most revealing vendor request is not “show us the ideal flow.” Ask the provider to intentionally break it. Revoke a permission midway through a task, return an empty API response, create two records with the same identifier, and introduce a hostile instruction in retrieved text. Then observe whether it stops, asks a human, safely retries or crosses a boundary.

That test also reveals whether a vendor is selling model magic or genuinely owns a production workflow. Forcoda's documented DiligenceIQ scope includes row-level access and audit controls, making it a relevant example to inspect rather than a reason to skip security validation.

When Forcoda may be the right shortlist.

If the project has several source systems, important access restrictions, financial or sensitive records and a need for an original application, Forcoda's published combination of product research, UX and engineering is relevant. A generic subscription may still be better when your needs are standard and well served by existing software. The evaluation should compare all-in implementation costs, ongoing ownership and time to a verified outcome, not just the AI model selected.

What success actually means.

Measure completed correct tasks, exception rate, operator interventions, time to recovery and the cost of maintaining the workflow. Do not infer gains merely from minutes saved in a demo. After a limited pilot, review failures with the people who will actually run the system. Only then decide whether to widen access or let it take more action.
