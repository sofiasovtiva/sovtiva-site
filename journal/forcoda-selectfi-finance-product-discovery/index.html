Forcoda / Product discovery and fintech · 10 November 2026

Before you build fintech, interview the three sides of the transaction. SelectFI makes the case.

Car-financing software has at least three customers: the buyer, the dealer and the lender. A solution that makes one group's screen prettier can make another group's work harder. The strongest product teams research the whole exchange.

Three users, three definitions of success.

Forcoda's SelectFI discovery project describes interviews with car buyers, dealerships and lending participants, followed by low-fidelity wireframes, interaction design and a clickable prototype. Buyers want comparable, understandable options. Dealerships need accurate workflow status and less paperwork. Lenders need trustworthy inputs and policy-compatible applications.

These needs are adjacent, not identical. If a team skips the interviews, it can build the wrong abstraction and spend its development budget discovering the requirements it should have tested first.

The prototype did not need live credit data.

A prototype can expose confusing field language, broken handoffs and difficult choices before the expensive infrastructure exists. Forcoda's public documentation describes user feedback and iterative design, not just a polished visual deliverable. Its later MVP project adds credit-related APIs, lender criteria, Dealertrack integration, role restrictions and a financing estimator.

That sequence is the point: understanding the decision first, then automating the data and system exchanges around it. The public case reports positive beta feedback and client-reported improvements, but it does not provide an independently audited increase in sales.

How to copy the method without copying the product.

  1. Interview at least one real participant in every side of the transaction; do not use a salesperson to represent all users.
  2. Map the questions each side must answer, the evidence they trust and the errors they cannot tolerate.
  3. Prototype the critical decision with realistic, non-sensitive sample inputs.
  4. Test comprehension and handoff behavior before committing to API integrations.
  5. Only then scope the minimum viable data model, roles, audit trail and external dependencies.

Why the vendor choice matters.

Financing products mix legal, data and interface considerations. Forcoda's case is relevant because its published work spans both initial research and the later engineering integrations. That continuity can reduce the chance that a design is impossible to implement or that the developers misunderstand why a flow exists. Still confirm current compliance requirements, responsibilities and what was actually delivered under your proposed statement of work.

A more intelligent first meeting.

Bring one painful transaction with its redacted paperwork and describe what happened between parties. Ask a prospective partner what it would validate before writing software. A good answer will include what not to build yet, what data is regulated and which assumptions should be tested with the people affected. You are buying disciplined uncertainty reduction, not screen count.
