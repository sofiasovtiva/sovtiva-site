Forcoda / Executive decision systems · 10 November 2026

The next business dashboard may be a conversation. StratoBoard is an instructive test.

Executives rarely need another page of charts. They need to know what changed, why it matters, what data supports the conclusion and who should act. StratoBoard's published design makes that a concrete product question.

The interface should start with a decision, not a dashboard.

Traditional BI interfaces reward people who know where a metric lives, how it was calculated and which filter to apply. An executive often arrives with a different question: “Why did conversion decline after Tuesday?” An answer that simply produces a bar chart moves the work, rather than completing it.

Forcoda's StratoBoard project describes a conversational answer with a short explanation, inline visualizations and citations to the underlying data. It also shows how a conversation might become a persistent dashboard widget or an investigation handed to an agent. The important idea is not the chat box. It is preserving the reasoning chain between question, evidence and action.

A polished demo is not proof of live-data reliability.

Forcoda explicitly says the interactive product walkthrough uses realistic synthetic data so buyers can explore without creating an account. That is a legitimate prototyping technique: the vendor can test the interface and navigation without exposing production data. It does not demonstrate production connector accuracy, actual alert precision or customer improvements.

Before a purchase decision, request a demonstration against a permissioned sample of your own messy data. The system should surface stale entries, duplicates and conflicting definitions. An executive tool that cannot explain which revenue figure it used can accelerate the wrong decision.

Four design choices that deserve attention.

  1. Narrative-first answers: provide a conclusion, then the supporting chart and assumptions.
  2. Inspectable citations: each consequential number should have an accessible origin and refresh timestamp.
  3. Cross-device context: phone widgets are useful only if the same KPI semantics follow the user.
  4. Auditable automation: anomaly reviews and follow-up drafts need visible run histories and confirmation before consequential action.

The public StratoBoard roadmap names additional integrations and native mobile experiences. Treat roadmap items as planned, not as shipped features.

What this says about Forcoda.

StratoBoard's published team spans AI architecture, full-stack engineering, UI/UX, product and QA. More importantly, the case articulates a product thesis about executive behavior, then ties interface decisions to that thesis. For buyers seeking to turn complex analytics into a coherent application, that blend of strategy and implementation is a credible reason to shortlist Forcoda. It does not make the firm universally superior to every analytics integrator.

The buyer's proof-of-value test.

Choose three decisions that leadership actually makes each week: pipeline intervention, margin exception and delivery backlog, for example. Record the current time required to find the answer and the number of human clarifications. In the trial, require a cited answer and a reproducible metric definition for each. If the tool cannot explain a metric without a data engineer translating, you have learned something valuable before scaling it.
