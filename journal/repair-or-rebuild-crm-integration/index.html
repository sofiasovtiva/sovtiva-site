Repair or rebuild a broken CRM integration?

Before paying for a new integration, find out whether the existing one is broken in a specific place or no longer fits your business.

A form stops creating CRM records. Salespeople start copying details manually. Billing receives incomplete customer information. A replacement may seem like the obvious answer, but replacing an integration before identifying the failure can introduce unnecessary cost and disruption.

Start with the failure, not the software

Choose one affected workflow. Write down its source, destination, trigger, required fields, last known successful transaction and business owner. Preserve relevant logs without exposing credentials or personal information. Establish whether records are missing, delayed, duplicated or incorrect. A successful API response alone does not prove the correct business outcome.

Three ways forward

REPAIRKeep the existing design

Best candidate when credentials, field mappings or a limited API change are the problem. Low disruption, but deeper defects may remain.

REPLACEUse a supported connector

Consider when a standard connector covers required fields, triggers and exceptions. Check licensing, limits and data export.

REBUILDChange the architecture

Consider when requirements exceed the old design, support has ended or failures are systemic. Budget for migration and maintenance.

What the symptoms can tell you

  • One expired credential or renamed field: investigate a targeted repair first.
  • Repeated rate-limit errors: check actual API usage, batching, scheduling and retry behavior before replacing the system.
  • Duplicates after retries: require stable record identifiers and a safe replay strategy.
  • No owner, no useful logs and recurring manual corrections: assess whether the original design can be made maintainable.
  • New systems, approvals or unsupported legacy components: compare supported connectors and a scoped rebuild.

HubSpot documents authentication, monitoring and rate limits in its API usage guidelines. Its limits vary by app and account. For a platform-specific change, see our HubSpot integration readiness guide. Do not assume every CRM has the same constraints.

Compare twelve months of cost

Ask each provider for implementation, migration, subscriptions, monitoring, support, staff training and expected manual exception-handling costs. Include an estimate for downtime if you have evidence to support it.

Illustrative example only, in USD: a $1,500 repair plus $250 monthly support totals $4,500 in year one. A $4,000 rebuild plus $100 monthly support totals $5,200. The rebuild costs $700 more in the first year and $150 less per month thereafter, crossing the repair's cumulative cost around month 17 if all assumptions hold. These are invented comparison inputs, not Sovtiva quotes or market prices; licensing, migration and failure costs could change the decision.

Require a small diagnostic before approving the full project

  1. Define the correct output for one normal transaction, one missing-field case and one repeated transaction.
  2. Use synthetic records in a safe test environment. Check permissions and avoid real customer data until authorized.
  3. Compare expected and actual records, including duplicates, associations and failed events.
  4. Agree which system owns each field and how competing updates are handled.
  5. Set acceptance criteria, a named maintenance owner and a rollback procedure before enabling production writes.

During migration, avoid two integrations writing to the same records unless you have explicitly designed for that. Keep a recoverable export and reconcile changes made during the transition. A rollback must address data changes as well as code. Availability of a test environment, export and rollback depends on the systems and plans involved.

Where AI belongs

AI may help categorize unstructured requests or summarize exception logs for review. Ordinary rules and software engineering are usually more appropriate for field mappings, deduplication and exact financial calculations. Do not let a model silently invent missing data or approve irreversible changes.

What to ask a provider

Ask for a written diagnosis, options with trade-offs, a fixed scope for the first milestone, acceptance tests, ownership of source code and credentials, maintenance terms, data handling and an exit plan. Require the provider to identify any independent specialist responsible for delivery. The right answer may be to leave a working system alone.
